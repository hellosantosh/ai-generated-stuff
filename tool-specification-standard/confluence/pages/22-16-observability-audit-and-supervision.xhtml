Two distinct obligations live here. One is operational: is the tool healthy. The other is novel and more demanding: is the agent using it well — a question no traditional API ever had to answer about its callers.

16.1 The audit record

TS-OBS-01 — MUST#A32A2Asolid2#F4F5F7#FFFFFF

Every invocation — successful, failed, denied, or refused at policy — MUST emit exactly one audit record containing the fields below, retained for the period named in the tool's recordKeeping.retention.
FieldContent
traceId, conversationId, turnId Correlation across the whole interaction, not just this call.
principalWho the action was performed as, and how that identity was established.
agentWhich agent, which version, operated by which business line.
toolName, toolVersion, descriptorHash The descriptor hash is not optional. Without it you cannot reconstruct what the model was told when it made the call, which is the first question asked in any investigation.
argumentsThe validated arguments, with fields listed in piiFields masked per policy.
outcomesuccess, error code and domainCode, or the policy decision.
resultDigestA hash of the result plus its size and record count. The full payload is retained separately where classification and retention permit.
approvalMode, who approved, when, and the argument hash they approved — or the decline.
entitlementsScopes presented and the decision made.
latencyMs, upstreamCallsOperational attribution.
TS-OBS-02 — MUST#A32A2Asolid2#F4F5F7#FFFFFF

For tier 3 tools the audit record MUST be sufficient to reconstruct the decision: the arguments, the confirmation exactly as the principal saw it, their response, the result, and the descriptor version that produced the prose the model read. "The agent placed an order" is not a record; it is a summary of one.

16.2 Metrics

FamilyMetricWhy it matters here
HealthInvocation rate, by tool and audienceBaseline; also the first signal of a runaway agent.
Error rate, by codeSplit by taxonomy code — a rise in BUSINESS_RULE_VIOLATION is a market condition, a rise in INVALID_ARGUMENT is a descriptor defect.
Latency p50/p95/p99 against the declared classA tool that has drifted past its declared class is lying in its governance block.
Agent behaviorSelection accuracyOf calls to this tool, how many were the right tool for the question. Sampled and human-labeled; the single most valuable metric in the estate.
Repeat-call rateIdentical arguments twice in one conversation: a loop, an ambiguous result, or an unclear error.
Truncation rateHow often results hit the ceiling. High truncation means the tool is the wrong granularity (§12.3).
Post-call correction rateHow often the user corrects the agent immediately after this tool returns. Points at a payload that is being misread.
ControlApproval request, grant, and decline ratesA decline rate near zero means the confirmation is being rubber-stamped and is not doing its job.
Entitlement denial rateSustained denials mean the catalog is showing tools it should be hiding (§10.4).
Kill-switch activations and durationReported to the board with the incident.
The metric nobody instruments and everybody needs

Silent wrongness rate: invocations that succeeded, returned valid data, and produced a wrong answer for the customer. It cannot be measured from telemetry — it requires sampling conversations and having a domain expert read them. Budget for it. Every failure mode this standard is most worried about, from an undocumented signed quantity to a stale mark, shows up here and nowhere else.
