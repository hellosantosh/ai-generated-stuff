10.2 Ownership and lifecycle

TS-GOV-01 — MUST#A32A2Asolid2#F4F5F7#FFFFFF

Every tool MUST name an owning team with a paging escalation path. The catalog MUST re-verify ownership quarterly and automatically move a tool whose owner cannot be confirmed to deprecated, then retired ninety days later.

This rule exists because of a failure mode specific to tool catalogs: tools are cheap to add and invisible once added. A REST endpoint with no owner eventually breaks and someone notices. A tool with no owner keeps working, keeps returning customer data to agents, and keeps being selected — silently, correctly, and unaccountably — for years.

TS-GOV-02 — MUST#A32A2Asolid2#F4F5F7#FFFFFF

A tool MUST return the minimum data that answers its question. Returning an entire upstream record because it was convenient is a data-minimization failure, and in an agent context the data does not merely sit in a response object — it enters a context window, is retained in conversation history, and may be forwarded to a model provider.

10.4 Entitlements

Entitlement scopes are named <domain>:<resource>:<action> and reused across the firm's API and tool estates, so that a customer's consent means the same thing whichever surface consumes it.

ScopeGrants
brokerage:accounts:readEnumerate accounts and read their attributes, with masked account numbers.
brokerage:positions:readRead holdings, cost basis, and unrealized profit and loss.
brokerage:balances:readRead cash, buying power, and margin detail.
brokerage:orders:readRead order status, history, and executions.
brokerage:orders:writePlace, replace, and cancel orders.
TS-GOV-03 — MUST#A32A2Asolid2#F4F5F7#FFFFFF

Entitlements MUST be enforced at three points: (a) catalog visibility — an agent is not offered a tool its principal cannot use; (b) the gateway, before invocation; (c) the implementation, against the specific resource named in the arguments. Point (c) is the one that stops a customer reading another customer's account, and it is the one most often skipped because (a) and (b) feel sufficient.

 Visibility is an entitlement decision, not a convenience

Offering an agent a tool that its principal may not invoke wastes context, invites a failed call, and — worse — tells the model that the capability exists, which is enough for it to describe the capability to the customer. "I can see there's a way to place orders but I'm not permitted" is a disclosure the firm did not intend to make.
