6Annotations, risk tiers, approval

Annotations are advisory — never a control

An annotation is an assertion by whoever wrote the descriptor. For a third-party tool it is self-certification. No security control may depend on one. Controls derive from the risk tier, which the firm sets, reviews, records, and signs. Where the two disagree, the linter fails the build and a human decides which was wrong.

The four hints — defaults are pessimistic

AnnotationDefaultTrue means
readOnlyHintfalse Changes nothing anywhere — caches, counters, search history included.
destructiveHinttrue May be irreversible or overwrite state. Only meaningful when not read-only.
idempotentHintfalse Repeating the identical call has no additional effect. This is what tells a runtime a timeout is safe to retry.
openWorldHinttrue Reaches outside the firm's controlled domain.

All four stated explicitly, even at their defaults — an omission means the reviewer cannot tell whether the author decided or forgot.

Quick classification

Reads only, closed domainT0/T1
Touches customer or position dataT1
Reversible, non-financial writeT2
Moves money or creates exposureT3
Cancels a live orderT3
Disputed at reviewthe higher tier
Cancellation is tier 3

It feels like undoing something. It is not: an order that does not fill in a moving market is a realized economic difference, and you cannot un-cancel.

Place a real order — this trades money

  Buy 100 shares of AAPL (Apple Inc.)
  Account ••••4471 · Roth IRA
  Market order, good for today
  Estimated cost      24,875.00 USD
  Buying power after  59,485.20 USD

  Fills immediately and cannot be undone.

      [ Place order ]   [ Cancel ]
  • Rendered from the descriptor's confirmationTemplate with validated arguments — the action, the account in customer terms, the instrument in words, quantity and money, reversibility, and the material consequence.
  • Bound to a hash of the exact arguments shown. If anything differs at execution, refuse.
  • Expires — 120 s default — and is single use.
  • A tool can never lower its own approval requirement through an argument, a description, or a returned instruction.

Entitlements are enforced three times

(a) catalog visibility — an agent is never offered a tool its principal cannot use; (b) the gateway, before invocation; (c) the implementation, against the specific resource in the arguments. (c) is the one that stops cross-customer access and the one most often skipped.
