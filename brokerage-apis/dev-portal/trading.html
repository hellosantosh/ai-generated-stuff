Trading

Placing, amending, and cancelling stock and options orders. Everything here requires the orders:write scope and an ACTIVE account — and everything here moves real money in production.

The order lifecycle

An order is a resource with a state machine. Understanding it is the difference between an integration that reconciles cleanly and one that quietly loses track of fills.

Three things that regularly surprise people

A 201 is not a fill. It means the order was accepted and routed. Terminal state arrives later — via the event stream, a webhook, or a poll.

Cancellation is a request, not a guarantee. The order moves to PENDING_CANCEL and only reaches CANCELLED once the venue acknowledges. A fill already in flight can still land, so a "cancelled" order can end up FILLED.

An order can be rejected after acceptance. We accept and route it; the venue refuses a moment later. That surfaces as a later REJECTED state, not as an error on the original call.

Placing an order safely

POST/v1/accounts/{accountId}/orders
Requires orders:write · Idempotency-Key header required

The body is a discriminated union on assetClass: EQUITY for single-leg stock and ETF orders, OPTION for single- and multi-leg option orders.

Validation order

Validation stops at the first failure, so a rejection tells you the first thing wrong, not everything wrong: 

schema  →  instrument tradability  →  market / session eligibility
        →  options approval level  →  buying power and margin
        →  risk limits             →  route to venue

Idempotency

ScenarioResult
First request with a key201 The order is created.
Replay, same key, identical body200 The original order, with Idempotency-Replayed: true. No second order.
Replay, same key, different body422 idempotency-key-reuse. Nothing created.
Key older than 24 hoursTreated as new. Keys are retained 24 hours.
Generate the key once, per intent

Create it before the first attempt and reuse it verbatim on every retry of that same order intent. Generating a fresh UUID inside a retry loop defeats the mechanism entirely, and is the exact bug it exists to prevent.

The failure being defended against is specific: a socket timeout on a market order is indistinguishable, from the client's side, from a rejection. The order may have been accepted and routed. Retrying without a key doubles the customer's position.

Market orders and gaps

A MARKET order guarantees execution, not price. On an illiquid name, at the open, or on a halt resumption, the fill can land far from the last quote. Prefer marketable limits for anything a customer would be upset to see filled 8% away.

Options trading

Option orders carry a legs array — one entry for single-leg, up to four for a combination. Multi-leg orders are priced and routed as one unit; legs never fill independently.

Approval levels

LevelPermits (cumulative)
LEVEL_1Covered calls, cash-secured puts.
LEVEL_2Long calls and puts.
LEVEL_3Spreads and other defined-risk multi-leg strategies.
LEVEL_4Uncovered (naked) short options.

An order above the account's level returns 403 options-level-insufficient, carrying requiredLevel and currentLevel so you can tell the customer exactly what to apply for.

Open and close are explicit

Leg side is BUY_TO_OPEN, BUY_TO_CLOSE, SELL_TO_OPEN, or SELL_TO_CLOSE. The distinction drives margin treatment and position netting and cannot be inferred from buy/sell alone — selling a call you hold closes a position; selling one you do not is a naked short requiring LEVEL_4.

Two ways to get option sizing badly wrong

quantity is strategy units, not contracts. A quantity of 10 on a two-leg vertical is ten spreads — twenty contracts — with each leg scaled by its ratio.

Declaring strategy is not cosmetic. It is what makes the risk engine apply spread margin instead of margining each leg independently. The identical legs sent as CUSTOM get the short call margined as if naked, typically demanding an order of magnitude more buying power — often the difference between accepted and rejected.

Resolving contracts

GET/v1/instruments/{underlyingSymbol}/option-chain
Requires instruments:read · always narrow with expiration

Contracts carry a 21-character OSI symbol — AAPL  261218C00250000: six characters of root (space-padded), YYMMDD, C/P, then strike × 1000 in eight digits. Useful as a check on your own logic, but the instrumentId is what you trade against.

Amending and cancelling

PATCH/v1/accounts/{accountId}/orders/{orderId}
DELETE/v1/accounts/{accountId}/orders/{orderId}
Requires orders:write · If-Match required on both

Amendment uses JSON Merge Patch. Only quantity, limitPrice, stopPrice, trailingAmount, trailingPercent, netPrice, timeInForce, and goodTilDate are amendable. Changing the instrument, side, or leg structure is a different order. 

# 1. Read, and keep the validator
GET /v1/accounts/acct_…/orders/ord_…
→ 200 OK
  ETag: W/"ord-7-1755441062"

# 2. Mutate, asserting you saw that exact version
PATCH /v1/accounts/acct_…/orders/ord_…
If-Match: W/"ord-7-1755441062"
Content-Type: application/merge-patch+json

{ "quantity": "200", "limitPrice": "413.25" }
OutcomeMeaning
200Unchanged since your read. Amendment accepted; a new ETag is returned.
412The order changed after your read. Re-read and decide again — do not blindly retry with the newer ETag.
428You omitted If-Match. Rejected rather than defaulted, on purpose.
The race this closes

You read an order showing 100 of 300 shares filled and decide to reprice the remaining 200. While you are deciding, it fills completely. Without If-Match, your amendment lands against an order you believe is working, and the outcome depends on timing you cannot observe. With it, you get a 412 and a chance to look again.

Cancellation is idempotent — cancelling an already-cancelled order returns 200 with the cancelled order, not an error.

Webhooks

For server-side integrations that would rather be called than hold a connection open. Same OrderEvent payload, delivered by POST.

  • Signed with Webhook-Signature — HMAC-SHA256 over the raw body, keyed by your webhook secret.
  • Return 2xx within 5 seconds. Failures are retried with backoff for 24 hours.
  • At-least-once delivery, so handlers must be idempotent on eventId.
Verify the signature over raw bytes

Compute the HMAC over the body exactly as received, before any JSON parse and re-serialize. Round-tripping through a parser reorders keys and normalizes whitespace, which changes the bytes and breaks the signature — and the usual "fix" is to skip verification, which is how forged fill events get accepted.
