            Do I have to do this every Sunday?
            

              Yes — once per week, before step 2. Prices and company filings are cached on
              disk, and nothing re-downloads them by itself, so without this step the app
              would score companies on last week's closes and quietly hand you a stale
              basket.
            

            

              You do not have to remember it. The bar above turns amber, then red, as the
              cache ages, and Do the whole run for me always does this step
              before building your orders.
            

            

              To have it happen unattended, put the download in your crontab — then the page
              is already current when you open it on Sunday evening:
            

            
0 18 * * 0 cd /path/to/quantitative-stock-selection && .venv/bin/python main.py update-data

            Fractional shares, and what this list assumes
            

              The share counts are dollar amounts divided by the last close, so most of them
              are fractions. Place these as dollar-based orders if your broker
              supports them. If it only takes whole shares, round each line and accept that
              your weights will drift from the target — with a $1,000 contribution spread over
              30-odd names, a single $500 share cannot be bought at all.
            

            

              Prices are the last close, not what you will pay. Monday's open will differ, and
              the list ignores commission, spread and tax.
            

          


          

            Where the five numbers above come from
            

              
Portfolio value now
                  Every position on the My portfolio tab, multiplied by its last
                      close, added up. Zero until you record something. Cash is not counted.

              
Cash going in
                  Exactly the amount you typed in step 2.

              
Buying
                  The buy lines added up. In buy-only mode this equals your contribution,
                      minus anything dropped for being under the $5 minimum order.
                      On a rebalance week it can be larger, because money freed by sells is
                      redeployed.

              
Selling
                  The sell and trim lines added up. Always $0 in buy-only mode.

              
Portfolio after
                  Value now plus your contribution. Buys and sells move money between
                      positions, so they do not change this total — only the contribution does.
                      It ignores commissions, spread and tax.

            

          


          

            What the words in the first column mean
            

              
Buy newYou do not own it yet. Open the position.

              
Buy moreYou own it but hold less than the target weight.

              
TrimYou hold more than the target. Sell part of it.

              
Sell allThe model no longer picks it. Close the position.

              
HoldClose enough to target. Do nothing.

              
Keep for nowYou own it, the model has dropped it, but this is a
                  buy-only week so it is not sold yet. Expect a Sell all at the next rebalance.

Why these picks

      

        Every company scored 0–100 and ranked against the others in its own sector,
        so a utility is not judged against a chip designer. The five category columns are
        sector-relative percentiles. A blank means that factor could not be computed from
        the filings, and its weight was spread over the factors that could — never scored
        as zero.

        What these charts can and cannot tell you
        

          The cohorts are built from the companies this installation can price.
          That is today’s index members, so a company that was in the index on your from
          date and has since left is missing — and companies mostly leave by failing. The
          note under the table says how many are missing for your window. It flatters every
          cohort, and the wider ones most.
        

        

          Ranking needs a share count. Market cap is price times shares
          outstanding as reported in the filings available on your from date. A few companies
          report shares only per share class, which this reader does not yet combine, so they
          cannot be ranked at all and are listed under the table. Alphabet and Visa are the
          notable ones.
        

        

          A cohort is a buy-and-hold, not a strategy. Weights are set once and
          left alone, so a cohort that started as the 10 largest is not the 10 largest by the
          end. That drift is the point of the chart, not a flaw in it — but it means these
          lines are not what an index fund tracking “the top 10” would have returned,
          because such a fund would rebalance.
        

        

          No costs. No commission, spread, tax or fund expense ratio is
          modeled anywhere on this page.
